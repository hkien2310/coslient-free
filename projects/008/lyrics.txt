THE SLOWEST COMPASS (Track 1)

Leave the turnpike behind...
The mud is cold...
No whistle blows...

Red clay mushroom twelve feet tall,
Wet pine needles where they fall.
Two boots sinking in the loam,
No clock ticking, far from home.
Let them race for the coin,
Let them sweat at every join.
Flour on knuckles, soup in can,
Quiet drifter, broken pan.

They have sledges, they have teams,
Driving through their gnawing dreams.
We have water on the coal,
Not a mile to keep the toll.

Oh, the slowest compass will lead you through,
Not by running, not by the few.
When the whole town runs for a market prize,
You don't have to measure their sizes!
Let the wind blow cold on the ridge up high,
Keep that morning north in your own eye.

Old tin cornet on the rack,
Dented brass along the back.
Baker's caravan on the climb,
Heating dough and killing time.
Rusted joint upon the leg,
Salt and maple in the keg.
Slower step than any steam,
Truer walk than any dream.

Not one birch tree wrote a date,
Wondering if the spring is late.
Not one hedgehog broke a bone,
Running just to prove a throne.

Oh, the slowest compass will lead you through,
Not by running, not by the few.
When the whole town runs for a market prize,
You don't have to measure their sizes!
Let the wind blow cold on the ridge up high,
Keep that morning north in your own eye.

You were bent until your elbows ached,
For a promise that they faked.
Running fast to be the one,
Burning out before the sun.
Put your boot upon the stone,
Nothing here is overblown.
You are standing, you are dry,
Underneath this circle of sky.

Oh, the slowest compass will lead you through,
Not by running, not by the few!
When the whole town runs for a market prize,
You don't have to measure their sizes!
Let the wind blow cold on the ridge up high,
Keep that morning north in your own eye!

Let the stallions break their stride...
Let the wagons roll...
Two slow boots in the dirt...
Nothing left to prove.

Just breathe.
The road is yours.

QUIET THINGS GROW TALL (Track 2)

The kettle is hot...
Take a seat by the wheel...

Broad cap hangs above the dirt,
Shelter from the drifting hurt.
Boil the broth on charcoal coal,
Scrape the gravel from the sole.
Down the valley teamsters yell,
Waiting for the auction bell.
In the roots the lichen spreads,
Rest your forearms, rest your heads.

Twenty years of carrying sacks,
Straining on the carter's tracks.
But the fungus grows in night,
Without asking for the light!

Oh, quiet things will grow so tall,
They don't answer any call!
Deep root digging in the mud,
Feeds no empire, spills no blood!
You don't owe a single line,
To the row of houses down the line!
Sit your weight where old roots bend,
This is not the bitter end!

Strip of copper, tungsten wire,
Warmer than an army fire.
Caravan at rest among,
Pinewood needles soft and long.
Crackled varnish on the beast,
Eating on a crusty feast.
Flour is whitening the tent,
Not one penny wrongly spent.

Let the township count its gold,
Every man gets dry and old.
Not one cedar asks the crowd,
How to spread its branches proud!

Oh, quiet things will grow so tall,
They don't answer any call!
Deep root digging in the mud,
Feeds no empire, spills no blood!
You don't owe a single line,
To the row of houses down the line!
Sit your weight where old roots bend,
This is not the bitter end!

Forgive the days you sweated blood,
Carting crops through winter mud.
Forgive the lies you told your chest,
Thinking running was the best.
The oak inside grew five feet wide,
While you ran from tide to tide.
Nothing stolen, nothing gone,
You are standing in the dawn!

Oh, quiet things will grow so tall,
They don't answer any call!
Deep root digging in the mud,
Feeds no empire, spills no blood!
You don't owe a single line,
To the row of houses down the line!
Sit your weight where old roots bend,
This is not the bitter end!

The broth is warm.
The boots are dry by the hearth.
No whistle is coming.
No bell to answer.

Quiet things...
Grow tall.

ROOTS IN THE STONE (Track 3)

Leave the fog cover the ridge...
We know where our boots stand...

Heavy slate along the crest,
No one calling you to rest.
Burn the pine knot on the stone,
Not a word of complain known.
They build towers in the sand,
Counting coins with busy hand.
We have wool upon the frame,
Not one debt to fit a name.

They have horns and marble gates,
Trying to control their fates.
We have roots that grip the ledge,
Growing past the canyon edge!

Oh, put your roots into the stone,
You are stronger on your own!
When the cold storm shakes the tree,
You don't need their guarantee!
Let the fast crowd spin and fall,
You are standing firm and tall!

Patch of lichen on the oak,
Cast-iron kettle blowing smoke.
Snail shell chateau by the brook,
Put away the measure book.
Tin cup rinsing at the spring,
Doesn't ask the river king.
Walking steady through the rain,
Not one stitch of useless pain.

Not one crag has asked the wind,
Where its border should be pinned.
Not one mushroom in the night,
Begs a storm to give it light!

Oh, put your roots into the stone,
You are stronger on your own!
When the cold storm shakes the tree,
You don't need their guarantee!
Let the fast crowd spin and fall,
You are standing firm and tall!

Years you spent in crooked rows,
Doing what the market knows.
Waiting for a nod of head,
Eating on their bitter bread.
Now the hempen rope is tight,
You can sleep alone tonight.
No more debts and no more lies,
Look into the open skies!

Oh, put your roots into the stone,
You are stronger on your own!
When the cold storm shakes the tree,
You don't need their guarantee!
Let the fast crowd spin and fall,
You are standing firm and tall!

The kettle is singing...
The roots are deep in the rock.
No master to answer to.

Stand tall.
Your ground is yours.
