## 📜 CẤU TRÚC MÔ TẢ (DESCRIPTION — LETTER FORMAT v5.0)

Hello, my dear friends,

After publishing our last piece, I spent several quiet evenings reading through your notes. So many of you wrote about that heavy, familiar hesitation before taking a new step—the fear of stumbling, of looking small, or of losing what we've spent years trying to build.

A few weeks ago, while sketching a tiny puppet knight made of weathered iron and coarse burlap standing before a giant stone staircase, a simple realization struck me. We often overthink our journeys and count potential losses before we even begin. But when we look closer, a stumble rarely breaks us; it simply leaves a bit of dust on our sleeve.

In this film, "Step Into Air," you will follow the journey of a tiny bucket-helmet knight as he steps into an oversized handcrafted diorama world. When a wooden plank bridge snaps beneath his boots, he doesn't despair—he simply sits up on a soft bed of green moss, fixes his helmet strap, dusts off his cloak, plants a small twig marker, and keeps walking forward. Accompanying his climb is an original indie folk acoustic ballad featuring fingerpicked guitar, deep upright bass, and soaring chamber cello counterpoint at 78 BPM.

I hope this film serves as a quiet, warm reminder that you have nothing to lose except the fears you've created in your mind. Dust off your sleeve, fix your strap, and step into air today. Thank you for walking this path with me.
